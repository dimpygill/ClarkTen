💡 Course Overview

This course introduces students to the foundational principles of electrical and computer science engineering. You will explore problem-solving techniques, engineering processes, and contemporary tools used across the industry — applying what you learn in the classroom directly to hands-on lab projects.

We will examine current industry trends and their real-world implications, giving you the context to understand where the field is headed and how you can contribute to it.

Minimum Passing Grade: C or higher

Prerequisite: MATH 95 (grade of "C" or higher) or placement into Math level 60.

MATH 95 or Math Level 60 Placement
🗺️ What You'll Build & Learn

This course is designed to give you a broad but grounded introduction to both the software and hardware sides of computer science and engineering. You'll write your first programs, work in teams, tackle creative challenges, and get your hands on real circuits and digital systems.

By the end of the quarter, you won't just know the vocabulary of the field — you'll have built things, solved problems collaboratively, and developed confidence in both code and hardware.

Software & Programming

› Python fundamentals — variables, loops, functions

🛤️ How the Course Unfolds

Each unit builds on the last. We start with the tools of programming, develop the collaboration and thinking skills engineers rely on every day, then move into the logic and physical systems that sit at the heart of computing hardware.

📌 Note: This is a general sense of how the course will flow — not a fixed schedule. The order of topics may shift based on how the class progresses naturally, and activities may be adjusted along the way.
1

Unit 1 — Getting Started with Code

Python Programming

We begin with Python — the most widely used language in engineering and AI today. You'll set up your environment, learn how to write and run programs, and practice the core building blocks: variables, data types, conditionals, loops, and functions. No prior programming experience required.

🔑 Key concepts: variables, data types, conditionals, loops, functions, debugging, running scripts
2

Unit 2 — Working with Others

Teamwork & Communication

Engineering is a team sport. This unit covers how to collaborate effectively on technical projects — dividing work, communicating clearly, resolving disagreements, and presenting ideas to others. You'll practice both written documentation and verbal technical communication through group activities and short presentations.

🔑 Key concepts: team roles, active listening, technical writing, peer review, giving and receiving feedback
3

Unit 3 — Thinking Like an Engineer

Creative Problem Solving

Good engineers don't just implement solutions — they find them. This unit introduces design thinking, structured brainstorming, and iterative prototyping. You'll apply creative problem-solving frameworks to open-ended engineering challenges and learn how to evaluate trade-offs between solutions.

🔑 Key concepts: design thinking, problem decomposition, prototyping, constraint analysis, iteration
4

Unit 4 — How Computers Think

Digital Logic

Everything a computer does comes down to 1s and 0s. In this unit you'll learn how digital logic gates — AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR — combine to represent information and make decisions. You'll build and interpret truth tables, simplify expressions using Boolean algebra, and see how logic gates form the building blocks of real hardware.

🔑 Key concepts: binary representation, logic gates, truth tables, Boolean algebra, combinational circuits
5

Unit 5 — Powering the Machine

Electrical Circuits

We wrap up the course by connecting the digital world to the physical one. You'll learn the fundamentals of electrical circuits — voltage, current, resistance, and Ohm's Law — and understand how series and parallel circuits behave. These concepts tie directly to the logic principles from Unit 4 and give you the theoretical foundation that underlies all computing hardware.

🔑 Key concepts: voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's Law, series vs. parallel circuits, circuit analysis
